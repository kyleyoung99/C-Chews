## 2. The product (facts only)

**Savour** — swallowable single-chew tablets in a pocket tin, taken
immediately after coffee or red wine. Two SKUs:

- **Savour Post-Coffee** — cardamom & sweet cream
- **Savour Post-Wine** — dark cherry & sage

Flavors are composed to *complement* the drink they follow, not fight it
the way mint does. ~30 seconds. No rinse, no spitting, no sink. Chew and
swallow. 30 chews per tin.

## 4. The UMS — Snap → Melt → Wash

*Serves belief-chain step 4.*

### Long form (Template 01 / mechanism deep-dive)

1. **SNAP** — The bite cracks the shell, releasing actives exactly when
   residue is freshest and most liftable.
2. **MELT** — An endothermic erythritol melt cools the mouth and closes
   enamel's pores. `[SOURCE NEEDED — mechanism-of-action citation]`
3. **WASH** — A malic-acid-driven salivary flood — not foam, not mint —
   physically washes the oils and pigments off before they bond, then is
   swallowed.

It works with your own saliva as the delivery system, not a synthetic
rinse.

### Short form (Template 02 / Template 03 quick panels)

- **Snap.** Crack the shell, release the actives.
- **Melt.** Cool the mouth, close the pores.
- **Wash.** Your own saliva rinses the residue away — then you swallow.

### Central metaphor

> **"An eraser, not an air freshener."** Mints spray perfume over the
> problem; Savour lifts the mark itself, the way an eraser removes pencil
> instead of drawing over it.

## 6. Discrediting current fixes

*Serves belief-chain step 2.*

One failure reason each — for the "your mint isn't broken" section and the
comparison table:

- **Mints** — mask smell for a few minutes, do nothing about the residue
  bonding to enamel. Often clash in flavor with what you just drank.
- **Gum** — same masking problem, plus you're chewing in a meeting or on a
  date, which isn't exactly discreet.
- **Whitening pens** — target long-term color, not the in-the-moment
  chemical residue; nothing happens in the next 30 seconds.
- **Mouthwash** — needs a sink, a spit, and privacy you don't have at a
  client table or a bar.
- **Brushing harder** — same sink problem, plus it's abrasive and doesn't
  address a reaction that's already happened.

## 7. Discovery story

*Serves belief-chain steps 3–4 (credibility/origin).*

Someone in a client-facing job, mid-meeting, realizing their last coffee
was still working against them — reaching for mint after mint and
realizing each one just added a second, competing flavor on top of the
first. The question that started the R&D wasn't "how do we make breath
smell better" — it was "what is coffee actually doing to your mouth
chemically, and how do you reverse it in the time you actually have: the
walk back to your desk, the gap between courses." Landed on a chew, not a
rinse or a strip, because it had to work anywhere, without a sink, in
under a minute.

## 10. The nine objections, with answers

Every template must answer all nine. Answers below are the approved
language; adapt tone per template, not substance.

1. **"Is it actually safe to swallow every day?"**
   Yes — the actives are food-grade (erythritol, malic acid), the same
   category of ingredient found in everyday foods and oral-care products.
   `[SOURCE NEEDED]` See ingredient panel.

2. **"Isn't this just an expensive mint?"**
   A mint masks smell with fragrance. Savour's chew interrupts the actual
   chemical bonding of coffee oils and wine pigments to your enamel — it's
   solving a different problem than a mint ever could.

3. **"Will it work fast enough to matter in a real moment — mid-meeting,
   mid-date?"**
   Yes — Snap, Melt, Wash happens inside 30 seconds. See the 30-second
   proof / interactive timer.

4. **"Won't the flavor clash with coffee/wine the same way mint does?"**
   No — each flavor (cardamom & sweet cream for coffee; dark cherry & sage
   for wine) is composed to complement the drink it follows, not compete
   with it the way mint does.

5. **"Is this backed by anything, or just marketing claims?"**
   The mechanism is grounded in real chemistry — how coffee oils and wine
   anthocyanins bond to enamel, and how erythritol and malic acid interact
   with that bond. Formal citations are in progress and marked
   `[SOURCE NEEDED]` throughout this site until they're published.

6. **"Why do I need two products instead of one that does both?"**
   Coffee residue and wine residue are chemically different problems —
   different oils, different pigments — so each variant's flavor and
   formulation is built around a specific chemistry, not a generic mint
   base.

7. **"$20 for mints feels expensive — why not just buy gum?"**
   Priced per chew, Savour comes out to about the cost of a coffee-shop
   upcharge — and unlike gum, it's solving the actual problem, not masking
   it for four minutes. (Per-chew price renders live from `pricing.js` —
   never hardcode this number.)

8. **"It's a new brand and a new category — will it actually ship, and does
   it work as advertised?"**
   Savour is currently in `{{batchLabel}}` pre-launch. Joining the
   waitlist locks in the founders price and puts you first in line when it
   ships — no payment is collected until then.

9. **"Do I have to change my coffee/wine habits?"**
   No. That's the entire point — Savour exists so you never have to choose
   between the coffee or wine you love and how confident you feel for the
   rest of the day.

## 12. Belief chain — reference

1. This happens to me more than I realize, and other people notice.
   *(§5 above)*
2. What I'm doing now — mints, gum, brushing harder — isn't fixing
   anything, it's covering it up for a few minutes. *(§6 above)*
3. This happens because of a specific chemical reaction in my mouth, not
   because I'm not "fresh" enough. *(§3 above)*
4. Savour was built specifically to interrupt that reaction. *(§4 above)*
5. It will actually work, fast enough, in the real moment I need it.
   *(30-second proof, all templates)*
6. It's safe enough and premium enough to become part of what I already
   do. *(§8, §9 above)*

The mechanism

Snap. Melt. Wash.

Savour was built specifically to interrupt the reaction — not to smell nice on top of it.

1. Snap

The bite cracks the shell, releasing actives exactly when residue is freshest and most liftable.

The technical version

Mechanical fracture of the outer shell exposes the erythritol/malic-acid core to saliva on contact, timed to the window when bonded oils and pigments are least set.

2. Melt

An endothermic erythritol melt cools the mouth and closes enamel's pores.

[SOURCE NEEDED — mechanism-of-action citation]

The technical version

Erythritol's dissolution is endothermic, producing the cooling sensation while the surface reaction reduces enamel-pore permeability.

3. Wash

A malic-acid-driven salivary flood — not foam, not mint — physically washes the oils and pigments off before they bond, then is swallowed.

The technical version

Malic acid stimulates a sharp salivary response; the increased flow mechanically clears loosened residue, which is then swallowed rather than spit or rinsed.

It works with your own saliva as the delivery system, not a synthetic rinse. An eraser, not an air freshener.

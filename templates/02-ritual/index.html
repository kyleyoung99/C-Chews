The Thirty Seconds After

There is a window right after the last sip — before the meeting resumes, before the next course arrives, before the photo is taken — where everything you've had is still sitting on your teeth, doing something. Most people spend that window reaching for a mint and hoping. That window is the entire product. Savour is built to do its work inside it, not after it.

The chemistry

Coffee oils and wine's anthocyanin pigments bond to enamel within minutes of drinking. Savour's shell releases erythritol, which melts endothermically to cool the mouth and close enamel's pores, and malic acid, which drives a salivary flood that washes the residue away before it sets. Origin

The Question That Started It

It began mid-meeting, reaching for mint after mint and realizing each one just added a second, competing flavor on top of the first. Not “how do we make breath smell better,” but “what is coffee actually doing to your mouth chemically, and how do you reverse it in the time you actually have” — the walk back to your desk, the gap between courses. A chew, not a rinse or a strip, because it had to work anywhere, without a sink, in under a minute.

A Tin, Wherever You Are

Desk. Bag. Car. Kitchen counter. One tin lives wherever the coffee or the wine does — not a fix you remember, a ritual you keep.
